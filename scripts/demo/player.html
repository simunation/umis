What happens
    How to read it

    The world is cut into zones of cells. Each zone is computed by its members, which take turns minting its blocks; full nodes (diamonds) also take turns. A zone advances one generation per slot, and only once all eight neighbours have published that generation, so the whole board is one exact Game of Life even though no machine holds it.

    Every zone slot is proved with a STARK (Plonky3, FRI over KoalaBear): a zone's slots since the last beacon block are proved together as one batch proof, on an RTX A6000 (GPU tag) or on CPU cores. Each beacon block carries the zones' commitments; a commitment turns from dashed to solid when its proof lands on the chain and verifies.

    Recorded live from the nodes on one desktop: each zone's generation is read from its members and the validators; boards sampled from the network were checked cell by cell against one global Game of Life computed from the same genesis (the badge above). After a rollback, nodes briefly hold different views while they re-sync; the board then shows each zone at the generation its neighbours allow, as the canonical chain must.

    Edge cases in this run: gliders crossing zone corners (where four zones meet), a glider gun whose stream crosses edges, spaceships leaving the world through its dead border, methuselahs on junctions, a crashed and restarted prover, a member that joins late, and the network rolling back and recomputing together.
